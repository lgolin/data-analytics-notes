Warning⚠️ Time is the main risk

Professor’s note: “I’m going to ask you to use the auto.arima, because it’s quicker, and the main problem in the exam is going to be the time.”

Translation: don’t waste time hand-identifying p and q from correlograms if you’re stuck. Use auto.arima(), then justify why the result makes sense.

1 What the exam actually asks you to do

The exam mirrors the workflow Francisco walked through with the USAccDeaths dataset. Every analysis follows the same nine steps, in the same order:

# Step Why
1 Load + confirm class(AP) == "ts" A ts object is required for the time-series functions
2 Plot the series Spot trend, seasonality, changing variance by eye
3 First correlogram (acf2) Confirm seasonality and long memory
4 Decomposition (additive vs multiplicative) See trend + seasonal + residuals separately
5 Stationarity tests (ADF + KPSS) Statistical evidence for the mean
6 Transformations: log → diff → seasonal diff Make the series stationary
7 Identify orders (p,d,q)(P,D,Q) from final correlogram Build the ARIMA model
8 auto.arima() + checkresiduals() + sarima() Fit and validate
9 Forecast h = 2*p ahead Predict the next two cycles
Note🍎 Analogy — the workflow as cooking

Think of stationarity as preparing ingredients before you can cook the ARIMA “dish”: - Variance problem = ingredient is the wrong size → use log() to chop it down - Trend problem = ingredients are leaning to one side → use diff() to level them - Seasonality = the same flavour repeats every 12 portions → use diff(lag=12) to remove it

Only when the ingredients are flat and uniform (white noise) can you cook the ARMA model on top.

2 The three stationarity conditions

This is the single most important concept in the whole module.

# Condition What it means Fix Where it goes in (p,d,q)(P,D,Q)
1 Constant mean No trend up or down diff(Y) (first difference) d = 1
2 Constant variance Wobble size doesn’t change over time log(Y) Not in the model — done before fitting
3 Constant covariance Relationship across periods is stable (no seasonality) diff(Y, lag = p) D = 1
Important⭐ The order matters

Professor’s note (Zoom): “The first thing we have to do in an analysis is removing or stabilising the variance, right? So that’s why we are starting every time by taking logs.”

Why? ARIMA can fix mean and seasonality through differencing, but it cannot fix variance. You must help it with log() first.

3 ADF vs KPSS — don’t confuse the hypotheses

These two tests look similar but have opposite null hypotheses. Mixing them up loses easy marks.

Test H₀ (null) H₁ (alternative) What you want for stationarity
ADF (adf.test) Series is NOT stationary (has a unit root) Series is stationary REJECT H₀ → p-value < 0.05
KPSS (kpss.test) Series IS stationary Series is NOT stationary DO NOT REJECT H₀ → p-value > 0.05
Warning⚠️ Common trap

A small p-value means opposite things in the two tests:

  • ADF small p-value → “stationary”
  • KPSS small p-value → “NOT stationary”

Always read the printed H0 line in the R output before concluding.

Note🍎 Limitation Francisco emphasised

Both tests focus mostly on the mean (constant trend). They can both say “stationary” even when there’s a clear seasonal/covariance problem. Always combine the tests with the plot and the correlogram.

4 Reading correlograms — the cheat sheet

After all transformations, look at acf2(transformed_series). Apply these rules separately to the normal part (lags 1, 2, 3, …) and the seasonal part (lags p, 2p, 3p where p = 12 for monthly).

ACF PACF Model Order
Drops sharp after lag k Tails off slowly MA(k) q = k, p = 0
Tails off slowly Drops sharp after lag k AR(k) p = k, q = 0
Tails off slowly Tails off slowly ARMA(1,1) p = 1, q = 1
All bars inside blue band All bars inside blue band White noise ✅ Done
Important⭐ Francisco’s exam advice (verbatim from Zoom)

“In the exam, just pick one, right? If I’m asking you to identify a model, and you know that you have this — yes, select one, justify your answer. As long as you are able to justify with statistical thinking, the model that you are selecting is fine.”

Translation: if both AR(1) and MA(1) look plausible, write down ONE and explain in 2 sentences why the ACF/PACF supports it. Do not freeze trying to choose.

5 Validating the model — what checkresiduals() and sarima() show

A fitted model is only accepted if its residuals look like white noise. The residuals must be:

  • Random (no pattern in the time plot)
  • Independent (no spikes in the residual ACF)
  • Normally distributed (histogram is bell-shaped, Q-Q plot follows the diagonal)

The Ljung–Box test at the bottom of checkresiduals() and sarima() gives the formal check:

Element What it means What you want
H₀ Residuals are independent (ρ = 0) Accept H₀
p-value Strength of evidence against H₀ p > 0.05 ✅
Ljung-Box dots in sarima() One dot per lag ALL dots ABOVE the blue line

6 Forecasting

forecast(fit_auto, h = 2 * p) gives:

  • Point forecast (the dark line) — the model’s best single guess
  • 80% prediction interval (inner shaded band) — wider = more uncertain
  • 95% prediction interval (outer shaded band)

The bands always widen as you forecast further ahead — this is correct behaviour, not a problem.

Warning⚠️ If you used log()

You must back-transform with exp() before reporting the forecast in the original units. Forecasts in log scale are useless to the end user.

7 What to write in the Word doc

For every R chunk you paste in, the marker wants to see interpretation below it. A good answer for one R output looks like this:

[paste plot or output here]

The ADF test gives a p-value of 0.02, which is below α = 0.05. Since H₀ for the ADF test is “non-stationary”, we reject H₀ and conclude the series is stationary in the mean. However, the correlogram still shows seasonal spikes at lags 12 and 24, so we must also seasonally difference (D = 1).

Notice the structure: what the number is → which test/H₀ it relates to → the conclusion → what we do next.

8 Likely exam shape (based on the practical exam Francisco walked through)

8.1 Question 1 — Simulation (smaller weight)

You’ll likely be asked to:

  1. Simulate an AR or MA process with arima.sim()
  2. Plot it and the correlograms
  3. Identify which model it is by reading the ACF/PACF
  4. Confirm with auto.arima()

Practice this by re-running the simulation block at the end of the R template.

8.2 Question 2 — Full data analysis (larger weight)

You’ll get a real ts dataset and have to walk through steps 1 → 9 above.

You will be asked to:

  • Plot it and describe what you see
  • Decompose (additive vs multiplicative)
  • Test stationarity (ADF + KPSS)
  • Apply the transformations needed
  • Identify the model order from correlograms (or use auto.arima)
  • Validate the residuals
  • Forecast 2 cycles ahead

9 Pre-exam checklist

Important⭐ Three rules to repeat in your head during the exam
  1. Variance first (log), then trend (diff), then seasonality (seasonal diff)
  2. auto.arima() is your friend — Francisco said so himself
  3. Every R output needs ONE sentence of interpretation — that’s where the marks are
